# Recommendations

## 1. Prioritize downtown rebalancing before commute peaks

Stations such as King St W / Bay St, Temperance St Station, and University Ave / Gerrard St W should be treated as priority rebalancing locations.

These stations showed repeated morning dock pressure and evening bike shortage pressure.

## 2. Use different rebalancing strategies for morning and evening periods

Morning operations should focus on creating dock availability at high-arrival downtown stations.

Evening operations should focus on ensuring enough bikes are available before the 4 PM to 6 PM departure peak.

A single daily rebalancing plan is not enough because the direction of demand changes across the day.

## 3. Monitor high-risk station-hour combinations, not just high-volume stations

A station with many trips is not automatically the biggest operational risk.

The most useful signal is repeated imbalance between arrivals and departures.

Operations teams should monitor station-hour combinations where large positive or negative net flow happens repeatedly.

## 4. Use temperature-aware demand planning

Demand increased strongly as temperature rose.

During warmer winter and early-spring days, operations teams should expect higher demand and prepare more active rebalancing coverage.

This is especially important when temperatures rise above 5°C.

## 5. Treat precipitation insights carefully

Initial results suggest trips during precipitation may have shorter average durations.

However, weather impact should be evaluated using trips per weather hour rather than total trips alone.

This prevents misleading conclusions caused by unequal numbers of rainy and non-rainy hours.

## 6. Improve future analysis with live station availability data

This project uses station-flow imbalance as a proxy for shortage risk.

A future version should collect live station status data, including:

- bikes available
- docks available
- station empty status
- station full status

This would allow the analysis to measure actual bike and dock shortages instead of only estimating shortage pressure.
